If you cannot hear callers through your phone's earpiece speaker unless you switch to speakerphone or plug in headphones, the earpiece channel is either physically blocked by compacted skin oils and dust, or the phone's audio output is digitally routed to an external device. Before seeking a hardware repair, you can usually restore call audio by cleaning the ultra-fine earpiece mesh, disconnecting hidden Bluetooth audio streams, and resetting call audio routing settings.

The earpiece speaker sits at the top edge of your smartphone glass, using an extremely narrow slit that collects makeup, facial oils, and dust every time you press the phone to your ear.

## Quick fix steps

Follow these targeted steps to diagnose and resolve earpiece audio failures:

1. **Turn up in-call volume during an active call.** Phones adjust in-call earpiece volume independently from media volume. Place a test call, hold the phone to your ear, and press Volume Up repeatedly until it reaches maximum.
2. **Toggle speakerphone on and off.** While on a call, tap Speaker to route audio to the bottom loudspeaker, then tap it again to return to the earpiece. This forces the audio service to re-initialize the top receiver channel.
3. **Turn off Bluetooth to disconnect hidden audio devices.** Phones often stay paired to a car audio system, TV, or earbuds in another room. Disable Bluetooth in Quick Settings or Control Center to ensure call audio is not transmitting elsewhere.
4. **Inspect and clean the top earpiece slit.** Shine a light across the top bezel. If the mesh looks clogged, take a clean, dry, soft-bristled toothbrush and gently brush horizontally across the slit without pressing in.
5. **Use low-frequency vibrations to dislodge grime.** Open our free [Fix Android Speaker tool](/fix-android-speaker) or [Fix iPhone Speaker tool](/fix-iphone-speaker). Playing a 165Hz tone at maximum volume causes the receiver diaphragm to oscillate rapidly, helping loosen dried oils and dust trapped in microscopic pores.
6. **Check Call Audio Routing settings (iPhone).** On iPhone, go to **Settings > Accessibility > Touch > Call Audio Routing** and ensure it is set to "Automatic" rather than a headset profile.
7. **Remove misaligned screen protectors or cases.** Inexpensive glass protectors often lack precise cutouts or slide downward, blocking the acoustic aperture completely.

## Why it happens

Smartphones use two separate speaker assemblies: the main loudspeaker at the bottom for media and speakerphone, and the top receiver (earpiece) for private calls. Several issues affect the earpiece specifically:

- **Accumulated facial oils and makeup:** Every time you hold your phone to your ear, your skin deposits traces of sebum, cosmetics, and sweat onto the mesh. Over months, these substances mix with dust to form a dense plug across the tiny perforations.
- **Water droplets trapped by surface tension:** During rain or workouts, moisture can wick into the narrow slit. Capillary action holds liquid tight against the mesh, cutting off speech frequencies until evaporated.
- **Audio routing state conflicts:** Mobile operating systems dynamically route voice calls across cellular networks, VoLTE, and Bluetooth. If a communication daemon hangs or misinterprets a disconnected headset as present, call audio is piped to a dead channel.
- **Proximity sensor failure:** Phones use a sensor near the earpiece to detect when the phone is held against your face. If the sensor fails or is covered in grime, the phone may fail to switch audio modes correctly.

## Unsafe cleaning methods that cause damage

Because the earpiece slit sits directly beside sensitive front camera and Face ID components, aggressive cleaning is hazardous:

- **Do not insert needles, safety pins, or paper clips.** Metal tips easily puncture the delicate mesh and damage the underlying speaker cone.
- **Do not spray liquids or rubbing alcohol into the slit.** Liquids seep behind display glass, causing permanent screen discoloration and damaging internal sensors.
- **Do not use compressed air.** Blasting canned air into the earpiece forces debris and moisture past water seals directly into the display housing.
- **Avoid pressing adhesive putty too hard.** Putty can break off inside the microscopic slit, creating an obstruction that requires screen disassembly to remove.

## When it's real damage (and when to get repairs)

If cleaning, volume checks, and software resets fail to restore earpiece sound, hardware failure is likely. Contact an authorized repair provider if:

- **Call audio is completely silent while speakerphone works.** If speakerphone works perfectly but the top receiver emits zero sound, the receiver coil may be burnt or disconnected.
- **Loud buzzing distortion occurs at low volume.** If voices sound like harsh metallic buzzes regardless of signal, the receiver diaphragm is physically torn.
- **Audio stopped after dropping the phone.** Hard impacts can dislodge spring contacts connecting the earpiece speaker to the logic board.
- **Water submersion was followed by receiver failure.** Water entering the top seam can corrode the delicate flex cable assembly.

On modern smartphones, the earpiece speaker is often integrated into the display assembly alongside optical sensors, requiring authorized service to retain sensor calibration.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why can I only hear calls on speakerphone but not through the earpiece?
Your phone uses two different physical speakers. The bottom loudspeaker handles speakerphone calls, while the top earpiece receiver handles normal calls. If only speakerphone works, the top receiver is either clogged with dirt, suffering from an audio routing glitch, or physically damaged.

### Can sweat damage the earpiece speaker during workouts?
Yes. Sweat contains salt that seeps into the top speaker mesh. As sweat dries, salt crystals form across mesh openings, blocking sound waves and potentially corroding internal wiring over time.

### How do I know if my screen protector is blocking the earpiece?
Look closely at the top edge of your phone under good lighting. If the protector glass overlaps any part of the dark mesh slit, it obstructs audio. Testing call volume with the case or protector removed will verify.

### Can a weak network signal make the earpiece sound dead?
Yes. Poor cellular coverage or issues with VoLTE and Wi-Fi Calling can cause one-way audio where you cannot hear the caller. Test by making a voice call through WhatsApp or FaceTime over Wi-Fi.

The first 10 minutes after your phone falls in water determine whether it survives intact or suffers permanent electrical short-circuits. Retrieve the device immediately, power it off if submerged, disconnect all cables, and wipe the exterior dry with a clean cloth. Do not plug it into a charger or bury it in rice, as both actions accelerate short-circuits and port contamination.

Water damage happens because dissolved minerals in water conduct electricity, bridging pins on circuit boards and causing instant short circuits or rapid electrochemical corrosion.

## Quick fix steps

Act quickly and execute these seven steps within the first ten minutes:

1. **Pull the phone out of the liquid immediately.** Every second submerged increases the static water pressure against internal rubber gaskets and acoustic mesh seals.
2. **Power down the phone immediately.** If the phone was submerged, shut it off completely. Turning the phone off stops active electrical current from circulating through wet circuits, preventing electrolysis and solder shorts.
3. **Strip off the case, accessories, and SIM tray.** Remove external cases and bumpers immediately. Cases trap liquid against seams and speaker grilles. If your device has a removable SIM tray, eject it and pat the tray dry.
4. **Dry the exterior thoroughly with a lint-free cloth.** Use a clean microfiber towel to dry the screen, rear glass, and frame. Carefully dab around speaker openings and the charging port to absorb pooled surface liquid.
5. **Tap the phone gently to expel water from ports.** Hold the device vertically with the charging port and speaker grilles facing downward. As [Apple Support recommends](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102643), gently tap the phone against the heel of your hand to shake loose water drops held by surface tension.
6. **Rinse with fresh tap water if dropped in salt or pool water.** Both [Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108039) and [Samsung](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS00077611/) state that if your phone contacts non-fresh liquids (sea water, pool water, soda), gently rinse the affected area with fresh tap water, then wipe it dry. Salt and chemicals form corrosive crystals as they dry.
7. **Position the device under cool, moving air.** Prop your phone upright with the ports facing down in front of a household desk fan blowing cool air across the openings.

## Why it happens

Modern flagship smartphones carry water resistance ratings certified under [IEC standard 60529 (IP67 or IP68)](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108039). These ratings mean the phone is engineered to resist freshwater immersion at specified depths (usually 1.5 to 6 meters) for up to 30 minutes in static lab conditions. However:

- **Water resistance is not waterproof:** Resistance relies on precision rubber O-rings, pressure-sensitive adhesive gaskets around the display, and hydrophobic mesh over microphones and speakers.
- **Seals degrade over time:** Normal wear, pocket friction, drops, and temperature fluctuations degrade waterproof adhesives. A phone dropped six months after purchase has significantly weaker resistance than a brand-new unit.
- **Electrochemical corrosion occurs rapidly:** Pure water conducts electricity poorly, but tap, pool, and ocean water are full of dissolved ions. When voltage passes through ion-rich water, metal traces on circuit boards dissolve within minutes through electrolysis.

## Critical mistakes to avoid in the first 10 minutes

What you avoid doing is just as important as what you do:

- **Do not put your phone in rice.** Apple, Samsung, and Google all advise against rice. Rice absorbs water far slower than open air, blocks ventilation, and sheds starch powder into damp ports, creating a gummy paste.
- **Do not use a hairdryer, heat gun, or oven.** Heat warps internal water-resistant adhesives and causes lithium-ion batteries to overheat or swell.
- **Do not plug in the charging cable.** Connecting a charger sends voltage through damp connector pins, causing instant corrosion and burning out charging circuits.
- **Do not shake the phone violently.** Vigorous shaking forces pooled water deeper into internal cavities past speaker membranes.
- **Do not poke swabs or toothpicks into ports.** Cotton swabs shed damp fibers inside ports, and sharp items can bend charging pins.

## When it's real damage (and when to get repairs)

Even with prompt action, liquid may breach internal seals. Seek authorized professional service if you observe:

- **The phone refuses to power on or boot-loops.** A device that cannot boot or restarts spontaneously indicates a shorted power management chip or damaged battery connection.
- **Display abnormalities.** Green lines, flickering, dark patches, or condensation behind the glass mean water breached the display panel assembly.
- **Persistent liquid detection alerts.** If the charging alert remains on after 24 to 48 hours of fan drying, internal charging contacts have corroded.
- **Unusual heat generation.** If the phone feels warm while powered off, disconnect it from accessories and place it on a fire-safe surface immediately.

Manufacturer warranties explicitly exclude liquid damage. If repairs are necessary, technicians inspect internal Liquid Contact Indicators (LCIs) to determine out-of-warranty replacement options.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I turn my phone on immediately to see if it works?
No. If water reached internal circuits, powering up the system sends current through wet traces, triggering shorts that destroy components that were otherwise salvageable. Wait until the phone has thoroughly dried.

### Does wireless charging work if the USB port is wet?
While wireless charging bypasses the physical port, make sure the back glass and charging coils are completely dry before placing the phone on a pad. Charging creates warmth, which can cause internal moisture to vaporize and condense on camera sensors.

### How long should I let a wet phone dry before turning it on?
Manufacturers recommend allowing at least 5 to 24 hours of drying in an area with good air circulation before powering on or connecting a charging cable.

### Is salt water more dangerous than fresh water?
Yes, significantly. Salt water is highly conductive and corrosive. If dropped in salt water, rinse the phone gently with fresh tap water immediately to wash away salt before drying, as dried salt crystals permanently corrode metal connections.
